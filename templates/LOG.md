# Signal log: Product name

**Question:** what was observed, and when?

**One single log for all signals.** There is no separate "feedback" document: `type` is
what distinguishes them. The reason is not to save a file, it is to give change intake
**one single source** instead of two fighting each other for priority.

**Append-only, and this has an important consequence.** At the moment of observation you
do not know the cause, the remedy, or the change it will generate. You do not update the
row, you add linked events.

```
SIG-014   signal observed
ANA-014   analysis of SIG-014
DEC-031   decision taken on ANA-014
CHG-052   change generated by DEC-031
```

## Analysis

Only for the signals that require one. A full post-mortem only for significant events.

### ANA-NNN · on SIG-NNN

Cause · how we worked it out · what would have let us catch it sooner · reference to the
`DEC` if a decision came out of it.

## Verbatim feedback

For `type: feedback` signals, the original text **in the words of the person who said it**.

The value lies in not translating it into a requirement straight away: the original words
are the only thing that lets you re-interpret a signal six months later, once you
understand the domain better. A requirement already translated has lost information
irreversibly.

## Anti-patterns

- **Keeping it only for serious incidents.** Slow drift is not an incident and you will
  never notice it without a log.
- **An empty log.** Change intake will feed only on explicit requests, which means the
  voice of whoever speaks loudest, not what the system is signaling.
- **Updating a row with the cause you discovered later.** It breaks the append-only class
  and erases the time sequence, which is the only thing this document knows how to do.
- **Translating feedback into a requirement at the moment you collect it.**
